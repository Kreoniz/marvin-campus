# Mascot copyright and design review — 2026-10-20

Scope: the current Marvin Campus mascot and three proposed body revisions; a public, nonprofit university schedule app, primarily used in Russia. This is a source-based design risk assessment, not legal clearance or a prediction of litigation.

**Assessment:** the current mascot has enough recognizable features of the 2005 film Marvin that calling it safely independent inspiration is not supported by this review. Round eyes are a real difference, but there is no single-feature exemption. Moderate body revisions can improve differentiation while preserving the requested circular ivory head, mint eyes and two-arm thinking pose. Retaining the exact face and franchise-associated branding leaves material uncertainty.

## Evidence and provenance

Inspected `public/assets/marvin.webp`, `public/assets/mascot-mint-thinking-v1.webp`, the selected mint head, and the actual M1–M3 candidates in `.cache/mascot-two-arm-bodies-2026-10-20/`. The candidates have not replaced application assets.

[The asset record](../assets.md) says the illustrations were generated from owner-supplied character references and expressly does not establish rights in those references. [The retained prompts](../assets/mint-mascot-v1-prompts.txt) instruct the generator to edit the existing illustration, preserve the pose/body, and closely match the selected face. They also preserve the social card's “Don't Panic” and “42”. Thus AI generation does not establish independent creation. The origin and permission status of the supplied cartoon reference remain unresolved; that illustration can raise a separate rights question from the film design.

The visual standard was a **first-party production image**, not fan art: the [Jim Henson Company Archives article](https://www.henson.com/jimsredbook/2012/09/9131983/) identifies Creature Shop's work for the 2005 film and captions [this photograph](https://www.henson.com/jimsredbook/wp-content/uploads/2012/09/CShop_hhgg_grab_marvin5.jpg) as Marvin from 2004. I downloaded and visually inspected that image. [Disney's D23 film entry](https://d23.com/a-to-z/hitchhikers-guide-to-the-galaxy/) identifies the 2005 Touchstone/Spyglass film and Warwick Davis/Alan Rickman as Marvin. These production credits do not establish the current chain of exclusive rights or identify whom to approach for every possible licence.

This comparison concerns the **2005 film design**, not the earlier television costume. No third-party photograph has been added to the repository.

The most directly relevant guidance is **Supreme Court Plenum Resolution No. 10 of 23 April 2019, paragraphs 81–82, printed pages 43–44**. Recognizable parts can receive protection. Paragraph 82 considers the character's individualizing appearance, personality and other traits; reproduction can remain despite incomplete matching traits or inessential changes. It expressly separates this inquiry from trademark confusion. Paragraph 95 also recognizes that a similar but creatively independent work need not infringe; expert assessment may be necessary. [Official Supreme Court PDF](https://www.vsrf.ru/files/27771/), [official Rospatent repost](https://rospatent.gov.ru/content/uploadfiles/ppvs-10-20190423.pdf), [readable Consultant transcript](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_323470/ee07ed989eace60044215bd4b9b812f99a2f6919/).

Consequently, “the eyes are round, therefore it is not derivative and no infringement will follow” is unsupported. Neither a changed feature nor a percentage of changed pixels answers the legal question. The prompts evidence deliberate continuity with a reference; they do not alone prove that protected expression was unlawfully taken from the film.

## Visual observations and inferences

**Observed similarities to the film image:** a very large pale spherical head over a compact humanoid robot; a large empty forehead; two small green eyes very low on the face under one long dividing line; a tired expression; no conventional mouth or nose; pale rounded arm shells with contrasting dark joints. The film also has prominent shoulder shells and a segmented torso.

**Observed differences:** the app uses flat cartoon contours, warm ivory and mint, rounded half-open eyes with highlights rather than pointed triangular film eyes, and substantially simplified surfaces. The cheek-resting/crossed-supporting-arm pose is absent from the inspected film image; this review does not establish that pose as a film-specific feature.

**Design risk inference:** the concern is the combined face and character identity, not ownership of circles, green, or tired expressions individually. The round-eye change helps distinguish the drawing, but the remaining combination still makes a film-Marvin reading plausible. Visual similarity alone does not establish infringement, and this is not a court or expert finding.

All three clearly show the second supporting arm/hand. All retain the same face. Body changes cannot change the separate assessment of head-only header, favicon and installation assets.

## Practical direction and limits

Keep the requested circular ivory silhouette, restrained mint palette, heavy outline, minimal detail and two-arm thinking pose. A moderate torso/shoulder redesign such as M2 can make the body more specific to this app. For a stronger position, also reconsider the **shared long eyelid/dividing line, extremely low paired green eyes, and huge empty forehead as a combination**: independent lids, different eye spacing/placement, or a different relationship between eyes and face can preserve a sleepy expression without requiring wheels, robes or another wholesale silhouette. These are design suggestions, not a minimum legal recipe.

The name Marvin alongside “Don't Panic” and “42” reinforces a Hitchhiker reading in this context; this is a branding inference, not a finding that each short word or number is independently copyrighted. A separate trademark review would need actual protected marks, territory, relevant goods/services and possible confusion; no registration search or trademark clearance was performed. [Civil Code art. 1484](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_64629/7d076153e9adb1b3534c04eb371e2f7c6df713f8/).

Russia is a [Berne Convention member](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/treaties/parties/remarks/RU/15). The Convention protects qualifying foreign works, recognizes reproduction/adaptation rights and generally refers protection/remedies to the country where protection is claimed. A public website can raise other jurisdictions' questions; this review does not apply US fair use or provide UK/US advice. [Berne Convention arts. 5, 9 and 12, official WIPO text](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/283698).

A Russian IP lawyer would be needed to assess the final assets, reference provenance, relevant rights and exceptions, and any claim or proposed licence. Licensing the recognizable character is the appropriate route if retaining that identity is essential. The small nonprofit audience does not support a reliable enforcement-probability estimate. None of M1–M3 is cleared by this note.
